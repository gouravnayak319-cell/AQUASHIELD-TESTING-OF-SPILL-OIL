Scientific Framework

Data Sources & Attribution Methodology

A transparent multi-source architecture fusing satellite remote sensing, global AIS terrestrial and satellite streams, and hydrodynamic ocean drift modeling.

Satellite Remote Sensing

Primary detection relies on Sentinel-1 C-Band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) measuring surface capillary wave damping (Bragg scattering damping). Hydrocarbon slicks flatten high-frequency ripples, appearing as distinctive low-backscatter dark signatures. Optical multispectral sensors (Sentinel-2 MSI) provide complementary color contrast in sun-glint geometry and false-color hydrocarbon indexing.

AIS Vessel Tracking Data

Continuous ingestion of Class A and Class B Automatic Identification System (AIS) messages across satellite (S-AIS) and terrestrial (T-AIS) receiver constellations. Trajectories undergo Kalman filtering, kinematic plausibility validation, and speed profile tracking to identify suspicious slowdowns (typically 3 to 5 knots during oily bilge pumping).

Weather & Ocean Conditions

Real-time hydrodynamic advection incorporates Copernicus Marine Service (CMEMS) hourly ocean surface currents, NOAA GFS 10-meter wind fields (applying standard 3.2% windage transfer and Coriolis deflection angle), and wave Stokes drift. The reverse Lagrangian particle transport model solves the inverse advection equation to trace slick centroids back to release coordinates.

Attribution & Anomaly Analytics

Candidate vessels are scored across four weighted forensic vectors: Closest Point of Approach (CPA) distance to the hindcasted discharge origin, temporal synchronization between vessel passage and slick weathering age, vessel deadweight/type (tankers vs non-cargo), and anomalous behavioral indicators including AIS transponder deactivations and zigzag course deviations.

Managing Uncertainty, False Positives & Human-in-the-Loop Verification

Satellite radar images naturally exhibit low-backscatter phenomena that resemble petroleum slicks, such as biogenic surfactant films, algal blooms, grease ice, and low-wind calm water patches (<3 m/s). Furthermore, rogue vessels may engage in AIS spoofing or deliberate GPS offset manipulation. Consequently, AQUASHIELD enforces rigorous multi-spectral cross-validation, weather normalization, and requires human maritime expert verification before any formal regulatory or legal escalation.
